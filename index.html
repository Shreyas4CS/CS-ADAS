    From photos to a car that reacts

    Train mode walks through the same steps engineers use for real driver-monitoring systems.

    1. 1Collect

      Take photos with the camera, or upload them. Google’s face tracker finds the eyes in every photo automatically.

    2. 2Group

      Sort the photos into two classes and give them names. This is the “answer key” the AI learns from.

    3. 3Teach

      Teach four kinds of AI, one at a time. Watch each one learn, then peek inside its brain.

    4. 4Test

      Blink at the camera and see every model guess live. The scoreboard counts missed sleeps and false alarms.

    5. 5Save

      Choose the model you trust most and save it to the car. Then switch to real-time mode and press DAS.

    Won’t it go off every time I blink?

    No. An awake person blinks about 15 to 20 times a minute, and each blink lasts only a split second. DozeGuard times how long the eyes stay shut and ignores anything shorter than half a second.

    Alerts start only when the eyes stay closed for longer — 1.5 seconds for the buzzer, 3 for slowing down, 5 for a stop. You choose these numbers yourself in the safety rules. Many slow, heavy blinks in one minute are a warning sign too, so there is a rule for that as well.
